Temporal Validation Discipline A chronological split ensures the model trains strictly on historical data and predicts forward into the future, preventing lookahead leakage. Random k-fold cross-validation is methodologically invalid for transaction streams as it tests on the past using future data.
Feature Selection: Dropping Time Time is used strictly to establish chronological ordering and is dropped before modeling. Because partitions span non-overlapping intervals ([0, 120k], [120k, 145k], [145k, 173k]s), retaining Time as a feature causes severe distribution shift where future transactions are penalized purely for their timestamp.

Controlled Ablation: Quantifying the Time Distribution Shift

k held fixed at 10, full covariance, n_init=5 — only the Time column is varied
Fixed-Architecture Experiment (k=10, Full Covariance, Identical Splits)

To isolate the impact of timestamp distribution shift from model hyperparameter choices, we performed a controlled ablation holding the model architecture constant ($K=10$, full covariance, $n_{\text{init}}=5$, identical train/val/test splits):

Experiment ConditionInput DimensionTest PR-AUCTest ROC-AUCImpact on Fraud Detection
k=10 GMM with Time30 features (V1–V28, Amount, Time)0.26960.8942Timestamp distribution shift inflates legitimate scores; precision collapses
k=10 GMM without Time (Production)29 features (V1–V28, Amount)0.47360.9106+75.7% relative lift in PR-AUC (+0.2040 absolute improvement)
Mechanism of Distribution Shift In training, the GMM observes legitimate transactions with timestamps in $[0\text{s}, 120,396\text{s}]$. During testing, all incoming transactions have timestamps in $[145,248\text{s}, 172,792\text{s}]$ — several standard deviations outside the training support. Consequently, the model assigns high negative log-likelihood to legitimate test transactions based on elapsed recording time rather than transactional behavior, creating an influx of false alarms that degrades PR-AUC from 0.474 down to 0.270.
Engineering Takeaway Eliminating the non-stationary recording artifact restores clean density estimation on genuine behavioral dimensions ($\text{V1}$--$\text{V28}$, $\text{Amount}$), directly solving the precision collapse without requiring model re-architecting.

Model Architecture: Full-Covariance Gaussian Mixture

Multimodal density formulation & covariance conditioning analysis
Multimodal Density Estimation ($D=29$)

Legitimate transaction behavior naturally fragments into distinct behavioral clusters (e.g., routine small grocery charges, recurring digital services, periodic high-value travel). We formulate the legitimate feature distribution as a convex combination of $K=10$ multivariate Gaussian components:

p(x | Θ) = Σk=1..K πk · N(x | μk, Σk)
Score s(x) = −ln p(x | Θ)  [Negative Log-Likelihood]
Diagnostic Finding: Covariance Conditioning & Regularization Inspecting the fitted covariances reveals that 9 of 10 components have minimum eigenvalues at the regularization floor ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 10^{-6} = \text{reg\_covar}$) with condition numbers around $10^6$--$10^7$. Average log-likelihood on training data is $+31.42$ per sample. This indicates that local components are forming tight concentrations in subspace directions. This tight density boundary sharpens top-tier precision (PR-AUC 0.474) while explaining why global ranking across all thresholds (ROC-AUC 0.911) is lower than an unregularized single Gaussian.

Why PR-AUC Disagrees with ROC-AUC In 577.9 : 1 imbalance, ROC-AUC is dominated by the 56,887 true negatives. A single Gaussian scores ROC-AUC 0.959 because it separates typical legitimate transactions well, but its wide tail yields weak precision at the top of the queue (PR-AUC 0.148). Conversely, the K=10 mixture's multimodal density concentrates precision in the extreme queue, capturing 57 frauds vs. 21 for Mahalanobis.
Quantifying the Unsupervised Constraint The supervised ceiling (trained with fraud labels) catches 60 frauds at 49.2% precision under the 0.1% false alarm budget. Our unsupervised GMM catches 57 frauds (only 3 fewer) while achieving higher operational precision (62.0% vs. 49.2%) — entirely without relying on historical fraud signatures.

Threshold derived on validation set; 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (n=2,000)
Strict Validation Protocol (Zero Test Lookahead)

Thresholds are calibrated exclusively on the 56,961 validation transactions. At $\text{FPR} \le 0.10\%$, exactly 56 false positives are permitted across 56,904 legitimate validation records. The resulting threshold ($\tau = 114.75$) is locked and transferred directly to the test set.

Finite-Sample Variance in Test FPR (0.062% vs 0.100%) In the test set, the $\tau=114.75$ cutoff yielded 35 false alarms (0.062% FPR) compared to 56 allowed in validation. With an extreme tail budget representing just 56 discrete events over a 7.6-hour window, Poisson shot noise ($\sqrt{56} \approx 7.5$) and cyclical day/night volume shifts naturally produce minor finite-sample fluctuations. Crucially, the operating point remained comfortably within the 0.10% friction ceiling.
Addressing Positive Class Uncertainty With 75 total positive fraud cases in the test window, confidence intervals are naturally wide ([65.6%, 85.3%] for recall). Transparently reporting non-parametric bootstrap intervals ensures operational planning accounts for sampling variance rather than relying on point estimates.
